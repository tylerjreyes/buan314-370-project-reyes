# Proposal

## Problem statements

### Descriptive (what happened)

The San Diego County beach-program manager wants to compare bacteria levels and the percentage of samples above the state safety standard of 1,413 DNA copies per 100 mL across San Diego County beaches from May 2022 through November 2026. Each row represents one water sample, compared by month, days since rainfall of at least 0.2 inches, and whether the site is near a river, lagoon, or storm-drain outlet based on county records, to determine if the 72-hour warning rule should change.

### Predictive (what could happen)

Each morning before lab results come back, the San Diego County beach-program manager wants to predict bacteria levels in DNA copies per 100 mL and whether they will exceed 1,413 DNA copies per 100 mL. Using individual water samples from May 2022 through November 2026, the predictions would consider rainfall totals from the previous 1–3 days, days since rain, month, beach location, and nearby outlets identified in county records to help decide whether to post or lift warnings.

### Prescriptive (what should be done)

The San Diego County beach-program manager needs to decide how many days to keep warnings posted at each beach after rainfall of at least 0.2 inches, using individual water samples collected from May 2022 through November 2026. The goal is to minimize warning days when bacteria levels are below 1,413 DNA copies per 100 mL while keeping the percentage of sampled days without warnings that exceed this standard under 5%, using the current number of weekly water samples.
